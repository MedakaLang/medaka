── WHY THIS CASE EXISTS: it is what makes the pair discriminating ─────────────

Both impls share head tycon `Box`, so an arg-tag dispatcher selects "whichever
impl the import order reached first" — a choice that is INDEPENDENT of the
program. That has a consequence the corpus otherwise could not see: in any single
import order, arg-tag is WRONG for one of these two programs and ACCIDENTALLY
RIGHT for the other.

  program                    permA (ints first)   permB (strs first)
  ─────────────────────────  ───────────────────  ───────────────────
  ...literal-receiver (str)  boxint  ❌           boxstr  ⚠️ accidental
  ...positive-control (int)  boxint  ⚠️ accidental boxstr  ❌

So this is the ONLY case in this corpus whose untyped wrongness lands in permB
rather than permA, and the only ledger row on that side. Concretely, it guards
two things the sibling alone cannot:

  1. A reading of the sibling that saw only one ordering could mistake an
     accidental agreement for correctness. The mirror makes that impossible.
  2. A "fix" that merely flipped which impl the untyped fallback reaches first
     would drain the sibling's permA row while leaving this case's permB row
     wrong — the gate reports it instead of going green.
