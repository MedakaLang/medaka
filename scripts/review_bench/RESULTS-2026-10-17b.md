## What changed

- **Labels.** Every item a judge contested (194) was reread against the
  rulings. 119 labels stood; 66 candidates among them are now `reviewed`. 57
  items lacked the evidence a judge needs (mostly that a named symbol does not
  exist or has no caller) and gained refs or `fact` lines. The owner ruled on six
  open boundaries (R44 to R49), which moved ten items. Four `fixed` items
  whose fix left a defect of the same class behind became judgment.
- **Prompts.** The monad notes now follow R7, R8 and R9: the shared census note
  exempted mixed and differing-arm chains and suggested prefixing error
  strings. The core notes named two functions Medaka does not have. An
  after-fix control's few-shot "why" described the defect it no longer has.
- **Rubric.** Eight rows reworded to match their rulings, plus the six new
  rulings.
- **Harness.** A Haiku answer quoting `\{` is no longer a parse failure (four
  first-run misses were that). The report scores stored answers against the
  current labels.

## How to read it

- **The gain is partly selection.** Only contested items were reread, so a wrong
  label every judge agreed with was never looked at, and the pass leans toward
  the judges. Most edits added missing evidence rather than flipping labels,
  but this is not a clean holdout: several rubric rewordings were prompted by
  test-split disagreements.
- **The cascade still does not pay per item.** It dismisses 91 of 307 items on
  Jev's low band and loses eleven points of recall to save a third of Haiku's
  cost. The per-item benchmark asks only the labelled class; whether Jev earns
  its place as a router over every class of a hunk is a separate measurement.
- **Haiku's remaining misses are evidence problems.** `comment.stale` (7) and
  `dead.code` (6) lead; both need a caller search or the contradicting code.
- Per-class rows are still two to five items; read the pooled rows.
