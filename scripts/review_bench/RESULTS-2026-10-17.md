# First measurement, 2026-10-17

Reproduce at commit 8fbc5977f; the Jev and cascade stages were removed after it.

A frozen record of the first run: questions v1, fixtures as first committed,
test split only. These numbers describe that question text and that state; a
later edit to either moves them, so rerun rather than quote. Commands are in
`README.md`.

## What the run says

- **Haiku gets most of Opus's recall at a thirtieth of the cost** on these 40
  items, with the same false-positive rate. Jev costs a twentieth of Haiku and
  trails it by about 15 points of recall.
- **The cascade as fit does not pay yet.** It sent 199 of 315 items to Haiku,
  so it saved about 30% of Haiku's cost, and it lost 7 points of recall. Its
  thresholds were fit on two to five dev positives per class, which is too few.
- **Position bias is small for these yes/no questions.** Mean absolute
  difference between the two option orders was 0.025, and 4 of 315 verdicts
  flipped. Asking both orders costs little, so it stays, but it is not where
  accuracy is lost.
- **Context-dependent classes are weakest for every judge.** Recall on items
  whose evidence lies outside the excerpt (`evidence_scope` file or repo) is
  0.44 to 0.57 for Jev and 0.67 to 0.70 for Haiku, against 0.67 and 0.83 for
  local items. `dead.code` (Haiku 0.00) and `comment.stale` (Haiku 0.37) need a
  caller search and the contradicting code; that is stage-1 candidate
  generation, not a better judge.
- **The strong model's errors are mostly fixture errors.** Of Opus's three
  control flags and one miss, all four trace to the fixture: an "existing debt"
  exemption that makes no sense when every item is framed as a diff adding the
  code, a cost-contract control whose next sentence does narrate internals, an
  issue pointer whose role is arguable, and a positive whose evidence (the call
  sites) is missing from the excerpt. Disagreements with the strongest judge are
  the cheapest adjudication queue.

## Caveats

- Most classes have two to five test positives, so per-class rows in a fresh
  `bench.py report` are noise; read the pooled rows.
- 294 of 565 labels are miner candidates awaiting review.
- Jev questions are v1: one template across all classes with the rubric row,
  shared language notes and evidence in the state. A weak Jev class measures that
  state, not Jev.
