# Jev judgment tools: calibrated judgments between lint and LLM review

**Status:** PROPOSED, 2026-09-16. The census half is built (`scripts/jev/`);
the roadmap is filed as epic #3117 with milestones J-CENSUS, J-REVIEW, and
J-NATIVE. J-NATIVE is parked as of 2026-09-17 and section 5.1 says on what
condition it unparks. Tracking is on the issues; this document is the design
and the measured record.

## 1. The niche

Medaka has two ways to keep code in shape. `medaka lint` is mechanical,
exact, and gated: a rule fires only on a shape the AST walker can prove, and
the max ratchet keeps the tree clean of every gated rule. A full LLM review
pass (the `style-review` and `pr-review` skills) can judge anything but is
slow and expensive, so it runs once per sprint over a diff and cannot look at
the whole tree.

Between them sits a class of style question that needs judgment but not
reasoning: does this comment narrate history instead of stating a constraint?
Would these nested matches read better as a `do` block? Does this helper
reimplement a stdlib function under another name? The linter cannot express
these, and the crusade's own status record says the detector half is where it
is weak (#2276). Val's framing on 2026-09-16: fill the niche between the
purely mechanical rules and the expensive reasoning pass.

TypeSafe's Jev model is built for exactly that shape. It takes a JSON state and
a map of typed questions and returns only typed answers: a Choice picks one
option and returns a probability per option plus a confidence; a Noul returns
the probability that a yes/no holds; a Score returns a weighted position on
ordered levels. It never generates text, never explains, and answers each
question independently. Requests take about a quarter second and cost cents
per thousand. Live documentation: <https://docs.typesafe.ai/llms.txt>.

## 2. What was measured

Two runs, `jev-latest`. The first, 2026-09-16, sampled 160 items and had one
reader label each after reading it in full. The second, 2026-09-17, re-ran the
same questions and added 6 items. The labels are the expensive-LLM baseline Jev
is meant to approximate, so every number below is agreement with a labeler, not
truth. The corpus is `scripts/jev/eval_corpus.json` and the numbers are
regenerated by `make jev-eval`; if this table and that output disagree, the
output wins.

Two facts about the corpus that every number below depends on:

- **The 6 added rows are not a sample.** They were chosen BECAUSE the census and
  a cheap reader disagreed about them (5 `do` rows), or because a defect was
  found in a comment the census had scored as fine (1 comment row). Selecting on
  disagreement biases an agreement rate, so both figures are given: the original
  100/60 sample alone, and the full 101/65. The bias is small and runs both ways
  -- `uniform_chain` 0.949 to 0.955, `actionable` 0.986 to 0.973, `recommend` at
  confidence 0.7 from 0.88 to 0.84. **Quote the original-sample figure for an
  accuracy claim; quote the full-corpus figure when describing the corpus.**
- **The 160-row labels and the 6 added labels have different provenance.** The
  first are one reader's judgment; the 6 were adjudicated against the question
  text after both the census and its reader had been read and neither was taken
  as ground truth. The corpus's `_about` records this per row id.

### 2.1 Comment register (101 blocks, prose only; 100 in the original sample)

AUC is given as original sample / full corpus where they differ. Operating
points are identical on both.

| Question | AUC | Best operating point | Regex census, same sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| narrates history | 0.990 | precision 0.94, recall 0.94 at 0.75 | precision 0.81, recall 0.72 |
| belongs on an issue, not in source | 0.991 / 0.992 | precision 0.94, recall 1.00 at 0.80 | no class |
| register level 0 to 3 (Score) | rank corr 0.819 / 0.820 | mean error 0.47 levels | no class |
| reviewer-addressed prose | 0.908 / 0.910 | precision 0.75, recall 0.75 at 0.65 | precision 0.82, recall 0.38 |
| contradicts the code below | 0.91 | unusable: yes on 81 of 100 | no class |

The last row is the retired `mismatch` question and is **not re-measured** --
`jev_eval` no longer reports it, so that figure is the 2026-09-16 one and will
not move again.

**Scope, because these two numbers get quoted loosely.** `history` and
`offsite` are the classes that carry a strong claim: 0.99 AUC, and at their
operating points they beat the regex census outright. `reviewer` does not.
At the natural 0.5 threshold it is precision 0.54 -- 19 false positives against
22 true -- and even at its best threshold it is 0.75/0.75. A claim that "Jev
classifies comment register at 0.99" is true of two of the four questions and
false of a third. Name the class.

The history question beats the regex census outright and removes its known
false positives: the three instrumental "used to" hits in the sample
(`used to reconstruct`, `used to track`, `used to refuse`) all scored under
0.2. Calibration holds at the extremes and over-predicts in the 0.6 to 0.8
bucket, which is why the operating threshold is 0.75, not 0.5.

**The register Score's resolution collapses at the top of its range, which
the rank correlation hides.** `make jev-eval` prints the mean predicted score
per label level, and those four numbers say more about what the worklist can
rest on than the 0.82 does:

| reader's label | mean predicted | n |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.49 | 37 |
| 1 | 1.32 | 30 |
| 2 | 1.84 | 26 |
| 3 | 1.97 | 8 |

The levels are monotone, so they are ordered in the model's reading and do not
need rewriting on that count. But the 2-to-3 step is **0.13** against a label
step of 1.0, where the 0-to-1 step is 0.83. The Score separates clean prose
from bad prose and is close to blind between bad and worst.

That is a mechanical account of an observation section 8.4 could otherwise only
report: the 40 sites on the sprint's worklist all scored between 2.2 and 2.6,
and the blind control found the drain verdict close to constant across the score
range. The band is narrow because the worklist is drawn from the region where
the instrument has stopped resolving, not because the forty blocks were alike.
Two consequences, and only the first is about this question:

- A threshold on `register` selects a worklist honestly. A **rank within** that
  worklist does not carry information, so do not read the top of the list as
  worse than the bottom of it.
- The deeper limit is not resolution but subject. Register level is not the
  same quantity as "what do I lose by not opening this", and the two came apart
  at both ends of this corpus: `C100` scores 0.71 and cites a file that does not
  exist, while the 108-line block at 2.3 in section 8.5 held a live soundness
  hazard inside deletion narrative. Ranking by payoff is a different question
  from ranking by register, and is not one this question set asks.

The mismatch question failed as posed: given a comment and the code fragment
below it, the model treats any detail the fragment does not show as a
contradiction. It is retired (section 4.3) and #3121 owns the replacement.

### 2.2 do-syntax candidates (65 declarations, 60 in the original sample)

The existing rule `rule-bind-chain-to-do` in `compiler/tools/lint.mdk` fires
zero times tree-wide; the max ratchet exhausted it. Only one of the 60
sampled declarations is a verbatim passthrough chain. The remaining underuse
of `do` is the uniform-handler shape: every level's failure arm does the same
thing (`Err e => dieMsg e`, `None => False`), so a `do` plus one wrapper is
equivalent. That is the shape the shipped question asks about.

| Question | AUC | Best operating point |
|---|---|---|
| uniform-handler chain | 0.949 / 0.955 | precision 0.88, recall 0.97 at 0.25 |
| readability gain (Score) | rank corr 0.852 / 0.859 | mean error 0.55 / 0.53 levels |
| convert / partial / leave (Choice) | accuracy 0.67 / 0.66; at confidence 0.7, 0.88 / 0.84 | |
| convert-or-partial vs leave | 0.986 / 0.973 | precision 0.88, recall 1.00 at 0.80 |

Nearly every three-way miss is convert versus partial, a line the labels drew
by rule of thumb, and Jev's confidence on those sites is low. The other cluster
is two-level chains with a shared default, labeled leave, which Jev calls
partial at confidence around 0.3. Both are policy questions, and low
confidence is the right signal for a policy question.

**The three-way choice does not support an unattended workflow at any N.** It
is 0.66 accurate, and the 0.84 it reaches at confidence 0.7 is bought by
discarding 71% of its rows. Use the binary actionable split to rank, and let a
reader decide convert versus partial.

**Read that 0.66 against its majority-class baseline, not against 1.0.** The
labels are skewed: `leave` is 38 of the original 60 rows and 39 of the full 65,
so a constant answer of `leave` — a model that reads nothing — scores **0.633**
and **0.600** on the same rows. The question's measured accuracy is within a
couple of points of answering without looking. That is the real argument for
not running it unattended, and it is stronger than the one above; the earlier
draft of this paragraph printed 0.67 / 0.66 bare, which reads far better than
it is.

**Confidence does not separate the confident wrong answers.** Two rows in the
corpus are there to pin this. `D063` (`compiler/tools/lsp.mdk:815 typeAtPoint`)
is called `convert` at 0.82, above every other disagreement in the do half; the
source carries `lint-disable-next-line rule-bind-chain-to-do` and a four-line
comment saying the pure/IO nesting order is load-bearing. `D060`
(`gate_pack.mdk:2317`) is called `convert` where the tail of the declaration
cannot join the chain. In both cases the structural question is answered
correctly -- the chain IS uniform -- and the recommendation is still wrong.
**Structure does not determine the recommendation, and for `D063` the cheapest
correct signal was a lint pragma already in the file, which the question does
not look at.**

`C100` (`compiler/entries/profile_main.mdk:1`) pins the same point for the
comment half from the other side: all four register questions answer it
correctly (register 0.71, history 0.13, offsite 0.28, reviewer 0.08) and the
comment is still provably false -- it cites `perf_main.mdk`, which has zero
hits in `git ls-files`. That is not a miss. **A dead path is orthogonal to
every question in the set**, which is why it is checked mechanically instead.

### 2.3 The novel-language control

Measured 2026-09-16 over the original 60 declarations and **not re-run since**;
the figures below are that run's. The same 60 declarations were asked with the Medaka language notes and the
in-tree exemplar removed from the state. Ranking held: the actionable split's
AUC stayed at 0.98. The scale broke: every readability score inflated by about
one level, verbatim-chain false positives went from 6 to 36, and
recommendation accuracy fell from 0.65 to 0.57. Jev reads Medaka's shape
unaided; it needs the language facts in the state to calibrate what counts as
"convert". That is the documented TypeSafe guidance (do not rely on knowledge
in the weights when the fact can be supplied), and it makes the novel-language
risk a design constraint rather than a blocker.

## 3. Principles

1. **Code finds, Jev judges, the agent rewrites.** Enumeration is loose and
   mechanical (recall over precision); the model answers narrow questions
   about each candidate; code ranks and thresholds; a person or an agent
   applies the fix. Jev never generates the fix.
2. **Never a gate.** Every Jev tool calls an external API and answers in
   probabilities. It is a census or a review-seam input. It always exits 0
   and is never enrolled in `test/gates.toml`.
3. **Language facts go in the state.** Every question over Medaka source
   carries the relevant spec excerpt and an in-tree exemplar (section 2.3).
4. **A question ships only with a number.** Every shipped question has a row
   in the eval corpus and its precision, recall, and calibration recorded
   here. Change the question, run `make jev-eval`, read the numbers, then
   trust the ranking. The corpus stores each item's text inline, so it does
   not drift with the tree.
5. **Batch, cache, bound.** All questions about one candidate go in one
   request (TypeSafe measured 12x cheaper than one question per request).
   Answers are cached by content hash under `.jev-cache/`. Concurrency is
   bounded and the SDK's retry policy handles rate limits.
6. **Thresholds are read off calibration, not assumed.** The operating points
   above came from the calibration tables, and each differs from 0.5.

## 4. The style census: the question set

Owner: milestone J-CENSUS. Tool: `scripts/jev/jev_census.py`; usage in
`scripts/jev/README.md`.

### 4.1 Shipped

Comment blocks (every `--` block of two or more lines; state is the policy
sentence, the file, the block, and the code directly below it):

- `history` (Noul): narrates history or provenance rather than stating a
  constraint. Operating threshold 0.75.
- `offsite` (Noul): given the policy, belongs on an issue or design doc.
  Operating threshold 0.75.
- `reviewer` (Noul): written for a reviewer or narrating the change in
  progress; the criteria name the repo's own phrasings (`this slice`,
  `earlier cut`, `measured on this diff`, `see the report's Notes`). Weakest
  question; #3123 splits it.
- `register` (Score, four levels): the ranking key. Sorted descending with
  `offsite` and `history` as tie-breaks, the top of the list is the relocation
  roadmap for #2280 (#3119).

Declarations (every top-level declaration with two or more nested matches on
Result or Option constructor arms and no `do`; state is the file, the
declaration source, and the language notes with the `typecheckGate`
exemplar):

- `uniform_chain` (Noul): two or more successive levels over one monad whose
  failure arms share one shape and thread the success value.
- `readability_gain` (Score, four levels).
- `recommend` (Choice: convert, partial, leave). The ranking key is
  P(convert) + P(partial), threshold 0.80 (#3120).

Explored and declined for now:

- **Idiom quotas** ("use `|>` here"). The `style-review` DECLINED register
  forbids density targets, and a Jev Score would only make a forbidden demand
  cheaper. A candidate-and-vet shape (linter proposes a rewrite, Jev compares
  the two readings) is legitimate but has no consumer until `lint --fix` is
  safe on comment-bearing declarations.
- **Golden regression versus improvement** ("did this golden change because
  the code got better?"). The highest-value review question, but it needs
  reasoning over semantics, not a calibrated judgment over text. Route to the
  reviewer; do not pretend a Noul answers it.
- **Anything as a required check.** Principle 2.

### 5.1 Parked: the native Medaka client (#3132)

Parked 2026-09-17 by Val's ruling. Not declined: it unparks only if TLS is
prioritized on its own merits. Nothing here is a reason to build TLS.

**The transport was never the cost.** The endpoint is one POST to
`/v1/systemone` with a bearer key, JSON in and out, no streaming, no
pagination, no signing; the failure statuses are 401, 422, 429, and 529. The
client half of `scripts/jev/jev_census.py` is about 100 of its 514 lines, and
`stdlib/json.mdk` is already the right shape, floats and integers kept apart.

**The cost is TLS, and Medaka has none.** There is no TLS library in
`runtime/medaka_rt.c` and nothing above raw TCP in `stdlib/runtime.mdk`. No
program in the tree performs an outbound HTTP request end to end; the only
connect-send-receive path is the fixture `test/net_fixtures/net_loopback.mdk`.
The HTTP half is also thinner than it looks from the outside: `stdlib/http.mdk`
parses requests and builds responses, which is the server direction, so the
client codec is `pds/lib/httpclient.mdk`, and moving that out of the PDS
manifest needs the stdlib proposal path.

Four ways past TLS, recorded so the survey is not redone:

1. A loopback TLS terminator, the pattern `pds/Caddyfile.egress` already
   establishes: dial `127.0.0.1` and let a reverse proxy hold the certificate.
   No new code, and the Python dependency becomes a Caddy dependency.
2. `runCommand` over `curl`, about twenty lines, working today.
3. A C shim over libcurl reached through `[foreign-libraries]`. `String ->
   String` is in the FFI v1 crossable set, but function pointers and structs
   are not, so libcurl cannot be bound directly, `mdk_` names are reserved, and
   `test/diff_compiler_llvm_ffi.sh` is the only gate that links C.
4. TLS 1.3 in Medaka. The primitives are not the hard part; X.509 chain
   validation is, and no gate here can prove it correct.

**Why Python is the cheaper side of the trade.** Section 7 already quarantines
the dependency: the tool is not a gate candidate, always exits 0, and is never
enrolled in `test/gates.toml`. The hundred lines a native client would own are
the ones TypeSafe changes underneath us, and principle 5's retry behavior is
the SDK's.

**The half that needs no client.** The interesting part of J-NATIVE was never
the transport but enumerating candidates from the real AST instead of line
regexes: the current enumerator finds a declaration by matching an identifier
at column zero and counts `match` occurrences, which bounds the census's
recall. A `medaka lint`-style enumerator emitting candidates as JSON for the
Python half to consume needs no TLS, no client, and no stdlib proposal. It
belongs on its own issue if enumerator recall ever becomes the limiting factor.

## 6. Evaluation discipline

The procedure, and the tips for moving a question's signal, are the
`jev-judgments` skill (`.claude/skills/jev-judgments/SKILL.md`). This section
records the rules it enforces.

- The corpus is `scripts/jev/eval_corpus.json`: 101 comment blocks and 65
  declarations with their text inline, their labels (see section 2 — the
  original items are one reader's judgment, the 6 added later were adjudicated
  against the question text), and the regex
  census classes for the baseline comparison. #3124 adds a second reader.
- `make jev-eval` prints, per Noul: precision and recall at 0.5 and at the
  best threshold, AUC, and a five-bucket calibration table; per Score: mean
  absolute error and rank correlation; per Choice: accuracy at confidence
  tiers and the confusion matrix. The control is `--no-language-notes`.
- A question change is accepted when the eval does not regress the operating
  point recorded here, and this document's tables are updated from the output.
- When a census recommendation turns out wrong during a drain, the site goes
  into the corpus with a `note`, so the question is re-measured against the
  case that fooled it.

## 7. Operational notes

- Key: `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` or `~/.config/typesafe/api_key` (mode 600), created
  from a terminal, never through an agent transcript.
- SDK: `pip install typesafe-sdk`, or `TYPESAFE_SDK_PATH` pointing at a
  `pip install --target` directory on a box without a venv.
- Cost at the cut: about 1,700 input tokens and 0.27 seconds per candidate.
  A whole-tree first run is a few thousand requests, run eight at a time.
- Source leaves the box. The repo is public; keep the census off anything
  that is not.
- A `.py` under `scripts/` is not a gate candidate; a `.sh` anywhere is
  (`[WEB-SH-IS-A-GATE]`). The tool stays in Python; section 5.1 says why, and
  on what condition that would change.

## 8. Jev with a cheap hand: the 2026-09-17 measurement

Section 2 measures the *questions*. This section measures the *workflow* the
questions were built for: Jev ranks, a cheap model reads the ranked site and
proposes a disposition, a writer applies it. It reports what the cheap hand
actually produced across one sprint, and it proposes rather than adopts —
workflow rules are not settled in a design doc.

**N is 52 dispositions, one sprint, one repository, two models.** Nothing here
is significant at that N, no confidence interval is offered, and every
proportion below should be read as a description of what happened rather than
an estimate of what will.

### 8.1 What the claim covers, and what it does not

The claim is: **on the comment-register and do-syntax classes — the ones
section 2 measures well, at original-sample AUC 0.94 to 0.99 on the binary
questions — a cheap reader's KIND judgment is usually right, and Jev's ranking
produces a worklist worth working through.** In that scope, and only there.

Two precisions on that sentence, because the first draft of it was loose in
both. The `register` column that did the ranking is a **Score**, measured by
rank correlation (0.82) and mean level error, **not** an AUC; the AUCs belong
to the binary tie-break questions. And section 2's own rule is to quote the
original-sample figure for an accuracy claim, which is what is quoted here.

It does **not** cover:

- the `reviewer` question (AUC 0.91, precision 0.54 at the natural threshold);
- the retired `mismatch` question, which is not measured at all;
- the three-way `convert / partial / leave` choice, 0.66 accurate against a
  0.633 majority-class baseline (section 2.2) — that is, barely above
  answering `leave` every time;
- **any claim that the cheap hand's DESTINATIONS can be trusted.** They could
  not be, and that is the section's main finding.

"Jev plus a cheap model substitutes for an expensive reader" is **not**
supported by this measurement in the general case. What is supported is
narrower and stated in 8.5.

### 8.3 The do half — 12 dispositions, and one refusal that cost more than all of them

S-4 converted 12 declarations from nested failure-matches to `do` blocks, and
was written by the cheapest model on purpose, because this half has what the
comment half lacks: **a gate.** A wrong `do` conversion fails the build, the
snapshot, or `diff_native_cli`. The comment half is proved by nothing.

| attempt | outcome | acceptance checks | escalated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **REFUSED all 12** on a false premise | check 1 never reached | no |
| 2 | LANDED all 12 (10 `convert`, 2 `partial`, as proposed) | build, strict check on 4 files, failure-arm probes, snapshot, `diff_native_cli` — all green | no |

All 12 verdicts are `unchanged`: the cheap model's proposal was applied as
written and passed every check first try.

**The failure mode was a confident negative, not a wrong edit.** Attempt 1
reported that the language does not support the pattern it had been asked to
write. It does; four minutes with an eight-line probe refuted it. The identical
model, on the identical sites, landed all twelve once the packet carried that
measurement. The standing escalation rule — cheap model to a stronger one after
two failed rounds — never fired, and **would have been the wrong remedy**: the
defect was not capability.

This is the asymmetry the whole section turns on, **inside the half of this
sprint that had gates at all**: the twelve do-conversions (N = 12), where the
build, `diff_native_cli` and the snapshot would have caught a wrong edit. The
comment half had no such gate — section 8.3's own premise — so there the
asymmetry collapses and neither failure is caught. The refusal evidence is a
single episode (N = 1), which is an anecdote, not a rate. With that scoping:
**a wrong edit is caught by a gate where one exists. A refusal touches no file
and is caught by nothing anywhere** — it produces a
confident artifact, costs a full dispatch, and is indistinguishable from a
correct refusal unless somebody re-derives the claim. The cost here was one
worktree, one cold build, and roughly ten minutes; the correction was cheap
only because the reader distrusted the claim.

### 8.4 The proportions, with N stated

Verdicts of what was applied, against the cheap model's proposal (N = 52):

| verdict | n | share |
|---|--:|--:|
| unchanged | 31 | 0.60 |
| minor | 11 | 0.21 |
| materially changed | 8 | 0.15 |
| rejected | 2 | 0.04 |

**Read by population, because the aggregate hides the finding.**

- **KIND — what a block IS (delete / recast / relocate), or what a declaration
  should become.** Held on **49 of 52**. Two losses were owner rulings at a
  check-in rather than reader errors; the third, `c20`, was a **reader error**,
  and it was the most consequential site in the set — the reader proposed
  `delete` on a block that carried a live soundness hazard (section 8.5). The
  cheap model classifies well, and the one time it did not, nothing mechanical
  would have noticed.

  An earlier draft of this section said "50 of 52, both losses owner rulings".
  That was wrong on both clauses and was caught by review reading section 8.2's
  own table against the sentence, which is the only way it could have been
  caught.
- **DESTINATION — where relocated prose should go.** Held badly. **Seven of
  the eight** `materially changed` rows above are destination failures, as is
  every relocate-row `minor`; the eighth (`c20`) is the kind failure noted
  above, not a destination one. Of the sites where a specific *heading* was
  named, most were wrong on read: the heading existed and was about something
  else.
- **Two anchor populations, and only one of them carries a rate.** The cheap
  readers' own anchors: on the first slice, **3 of 3** relocation anchors were
  wrong or unverifiable — 0 for 3, which at N = 3 bounds nothing. Anchors
  pre-verified by a careful reader before dispatch: 17 verified, **4 later
  overturned** (13/17 = 76%) by writers who opened the destination. Only the
  second population supports a figure; an earlier draft asserted "both
  independently about 75% accurate", which read a 0-for-3 as agreeing with a
  76% and gave the pair a spurious precision. What both populations do support,
  qualitatively, is the finding: **verifying that a heading EXISTS is not
  verifying that it FITS** — and the pre-verifying reader made exactly the
  error they had warned the cheap readers against.
- **The ranking did not order — and a blind control says it may not separate
  either.** All 40 comment sites scored register 2.2 to 2.6, a 0.4-wide band:
  the instrument said "these forty are all about equally bad", and it was right
  to, since what was found inside them ranged from two dead lines to a live
  soundness note. So the score does not order.

  Whether it *separates* is the question a worklist actually rests on, and the
  sprint ran a blind control to ask it: 10 blocks, 5 drawn at register >= 2.0
  and 5 at register <= 0.8, line-matched pair-by-pair within +/-3 lines, read
  by agents who were not told which arm a block came from. **Drain rate: HIGH
  5/5, LOW 4/5.** A reader handed the register rule finds something to change
  in nine blocks out of ten, including four the census ranked among the
  cleanest in the tree. On this evidence the verdict is close to **constant**
  across the score range, and the 39/40 agreement on the anchored half is
  therefore not evidence that the census picked the right 40 blocks.

  N = 10 bounds this and does not settle it. But it points the other way from
  the rest of this section, so it is reported here rather than left in the
  sprint's own notes: **use the score to pick a worklist you are willing to
  work through, not to believe the blocks you did not pick are clean.** The
  sharpest instance is in section 8.5's own footnote — the blind arm turned up
  a provably dead path at register **0.71**, 138 places below the worklist's
  floor.

### 8.5 What a gate would and would not have caught

The single most instructive site: a 108-line block the cheap reader proposed
deleting in full, correctly reading it as deletion narrative and audit history.
About fifteen of those lines were a **measured, unreproduced cross-module
soundness shape**, carrying its own instruction not to fix it without a
reproduction. Same past tense, same issue numbers, same measurement narration
as the history around it. **A hazard note is textually indistinguishable from
history.**

Only a reader asking *"is any of this still load-bearing?"* keeps it. No regex
does, no ratchet does, and the register question does not — it is not the
question. This is the strongest argument in the record against ever making the
comment drain mechanical, and the reason section 3's "never a gate" principle
covers this workflow too.

The mirror case: a comment header that **all four register questions score
correctly as fine** (register 0.71, history 0.13, offsite 0.28, reviewer 0.08)
and that cites a file which has zero hits in `git ls-files`. In-register and
provably false at the same time. A dead path is orthogonal to every question in
the set, so it is checked mechanically instead — it is the one part of the
comment-register rule a machine can decide.

### 8.6 The proposal

Offered for decision, not adopted here:

1. **Let the cheap hand classify; never let it place.** A disposition may
   propose delete / recast / relocate. A proposed *destination* is a hint, and
   the writer opens it before using it. Prefer recast-in-place: a block about
   its own code needs no pointer at all.
2. **A pre-verified anchor must be read, not resolved.** Checking that a
   heading exists is worth roughly nothing; 4 of 17 such anchors were wrong.
   Either grep the destination for the subject, or do not name a heading.
3. **Spend the cheap model where a gate reads the output.** The do half was
   gated and went 12 for 12. The comment half was gated by nothing and is where
   every disagreement lives.
4. **Treat a cheap model's REFUSAL as its most expensive output.** Require any
   refusal that generalizes beyond the one site to carry a probe that was
   actually run. A refusal is the one output no gate inspects.
5. **Do not gate the comment drain.** Not by register score, not by ratchet
   beyond the existing count floors. The counts may ratchet; the judgment may
   not be automated.
