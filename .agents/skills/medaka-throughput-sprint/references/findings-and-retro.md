# Findings, refusals, and retrospective

## Findings

Open FINDINGS row before judgment: ID/handle, exact head, reporter/role, evidence path, affected slice/premise, provisional scope, status. Dispatch reproducer for minimal repro, controls, base-vs-head attribution, route/engine matrix, pin feasibility, and issue draft. Brain rules meaning: in-sprint versus orthogonal; orthogonal planned ownership versus gap. In-sprint ruling sweeps invalidated packets, landed assumptions, DAG leaves, and prior rulings.

Only conductor files issue, applies labels, reads it back, and records returned number. Finding becomes terminal only as fixed/reviewed, filed/read-back/pinned, false with evidence, or explicitly unpinnable with reason.

## Refusals

Maintain separate table: ID, role/slice, claim, adjudication, verdict, evidence. Include full refusals, stop-and-report, declined amendments, and falsified planner premises. Verdict: `UPHELD`, `OVERRULED (<measurement>)`, or `PARTIAL`. Absence of refusals is explicit zero, not missing record.

## Retrospective

Run after merge, using durable packets/reports/rulings/findings/friction/comments plus terminal `merge_group` CI. Reconstruct timeline from externally observed timestamps. For each finding, name catching channel: refusal, breaker/reproducer, conformance/light review, domain review, heavy repair review, CI, or nobody. Pull-request CI alone cannot establish merge-queue attribution.

Proposals require measured failure, smallest rule change, enforcement point, expected cost, and ready patch path. Name at least two retirement candidates with same evidence standard: why added, catches since, recurring cost, and cheaper subsuming control. Rules firing zero times across two sprints or subsumed by deterministic check are candidates. Retrospective never edits workflow itself; conductor applies accepted changes.
